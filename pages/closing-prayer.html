Beauty and Grief in
the Palm of Our Hands


by Sue Ariza



Having listened,
we are no longer neutral.

The question before us is simple yet difficult:
who will we be to each other?

May we begin with gratitude—
not the kind that comforts us into stillness,
but the kind that steadies us
for what is required.

We honor the courage it takes to speak
where truth is costly.
We honor the forms of care that arise
when systems falter.
We honor the wisdom carried by those
whose lives have too often been dismissed,
and the trust it takes to place that wisdom
in the hands of others.

We give thanks for intergenerational knowing,
the kind that lives in the body
carried across time
through story, song, memory, and touch.
For elders who remember what was tried before,
for young people who imagine what has never yet existed,
for the shared work of listening
that binds us to one another,
as an invitation.

Here, love is not an abstraction.
It is a practice.
A discipline we return to:
learning how to listen without haste,
to protect without control,
to imagine beyond inherited limits.

We are grateful for what slows us down.
For the patience to remain with uncertainty.
For the courage to live the questions together
not alone,
not in fear,
but in relationship,
trusting that attention itself
is a form of care.

We give thanks for community
not as an ideal,
but as something learned through effort—
through repairing,
making room,
and returning to one another
when it would be easier not to.

Because none of us becomes whole alone.
Because dignity does not fragment.
Because all flourishing is mutual.

We give thanks, too, for tenderness—
not as fragility,
but as strength practiced over time.
For the courage to dream together
of futures shaped by care rather than cages,
by belonging rather than banishment,
by systems that nourish our inherent worth
instead of managing loss.

We imagine a world where safety grows from relationship,
where accountability replaces punishment,
where freedom is crafted patiently
through shared labor and trust—
a world fluent in its own humanity.

Along the way, we learn to cup beauty
and grief in the palms of our hands.
Not to weigh them against one another,
but to acknowledge that neither can be carried alone,
and that it is the holding
that stretches us toward wholeness.

We learn to hold beauty without grasping,
to share it without fear,
to pass it on
so it remains with us.

We honor those who turn love into action—
into shelter, listening, witnessing,
and the steady refusal to look away.
Those who understand that love,
practiced collectively,
reshapes what once seemed fixed.

As you go on from here,
may you trust that your presence matters.
May you move gently with yourself
and faithfully with others.
May you stay close to what is life-giving.

And may we go on together—
grateful for our interdependence,
learning as we walk,
choosing again and again
to love with intention.

And may we let ourselves be filled
with wonder.
With miracles.
With the courage to act as if they are still possible,
as if our care can call them into being.

In solidarity, always.

Sue Ariza is a cultural strategist, filmmaker, and writer working at the intersection of art and social impact to shape intimate, dignity-rooted narratives that reimagine our collective future.
