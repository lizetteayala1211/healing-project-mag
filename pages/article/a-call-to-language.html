A Call To Language & Practitioners


by The Fortune Society



In 1967, the play Fortune and Men’s Eyes took to the off-Broadway stage. The play explored injustices across the criminal system, animated by the lived experience of its playwright, John Herbert. In response, the play’s producer David Rothenberg and activated, formerly incarcerated audience members collectively founded The Fortune Society.

With Art as our genesis, we are rooted in the belief that we should build people, not prisons. For over a half century, Fortune has been committed to using the transformative capacities of art, artmaking, and radical joy to imagine and advocate for future freedoms filled with dignity and wholeness for all. Over the years, we have evolved into one of the nation’s preeminent reentry service organizations, now serving over 18,000 individuals a year. Through our Creative Arts programming specifically, participants impacted by the criminal legal system hone their artistic practices, reveal or reclaim aspects of their identities, activate new coping mechanisms, and deepen their sense of belonging.

Like art, language expands our capacity for empathy. It can be a humanizing practice. Words shape and are shaped by our lives, experiences, and imaginations. They have the power to heal, grow connection and liberation, and reduce stigma. They also have the power to relegate and reinforce myths and stereotypes.

We would like to use this space to invite readers to use person-first language when communicating about those impacted by the criminal legal system. It is an opportunity to expand our community practice of choosing words that honor each other’s full humanity. The words we use to reference people should reflect their full identities, and acknowledge their capacity to change and grow.

We also would like to invite practitioners and the public to reach out to us if they are interested in collaborating with Fortune artists in some capacity.In addition to our staff, teaching artists and community partners facilitate virtual and in-person programming Monday through Friday. These programs include but are not limited to workshops on fashion and beatmaking, book clubs, open-mics, and theater courses. We also curate opportunities for participants to develop and share their work with public audiences and experience other arts-related events, including attending museums and galleries, dance performances, and Broadway shows. In 2025, we hosted more than 600 workshops and events, including our annual Arts Festival.

Should you want to be involved in building out opportunities for our artists either through donating your time/expertise, or donating funds to support our arts activities, please contact the Director of Creative Arts Jamie Maleszka-Tate at jmaleszka@fortunesociety.org
