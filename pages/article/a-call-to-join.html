A Call to Join Forces with Incarcerated Artists


By Parole Prep

As we write this, the crisis in New York State prisons worsens, and the efforts to suppress voices from inside grow. In 2021, the Department of Corrections and Community Supervision (DOCCS) issued a directive prohibiting incarcerated people from engaging with any outside creative and journalism program. Though immediate backlash to that directive garnered a reversal, the repression of incarcerated people’s expression has only escalated since.

In the wake of the Correction Officers’ strike earlier this year, correctional facilities across the state continue to keep people in virtual lockdown conditions. For many, this looks like 23 hours spent in-cell each day, cell raids and destruction of property, regularized violence, reduced visits, and no access to learning spaces or creative workshops created and operated by incarcerated people. As virtual programming platforms like Edovo now threaten to take the place of in-person gathering spaces in prison—further entrenching isolation in the name of “rehabilitation”—we need to be building partnerships across the wall. We need to be in conversation with the many forms of leadership and artistry that exist within prisons, and we must prioritize the return of our incarcerated kin to the community as a fundamental aspect of our work.

As part of Parole Prep, we are Archive-Based Creative Arts (ABCA), an NYC-based program that honors the knowledge, experience, and craft of artists, writers, and archivists incarcerated in New York State. We collaborate on publication, exhibition, and advocacy projects, connecting incarcerated members with community partners and supporting their work as it develops on their terms.

As Pamela Smart writes, our power to gather, to collaborate, to organize, and to vision lies in the relationships we build in spite of the aforementioned barriers and censorship. It’s how we resist when what’s deserved is taken away. It’s how we celebrate when we win it back. We call on the creative community of New York to recognize that many of its artists are currently warehoused within an increasingly violent system, and to join us in the fight for their freedom. If you are not intimately connected to people inside and organizations led by them, offer resources to those who are, and let’s begin building power together.
