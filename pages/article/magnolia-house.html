Magnolia House


by Meagan Betances



​​My mother wanted a magnolia tree. She fell in love with their large flowers. This was until she saw the mess they make when their petals fall, covering the surrounding area in mold. The last shelter I was assigned to before leaving the system was Magnolia house.

After my experience with New York City’s shelter system, I was left with nightmares. Though I was still homeless, I needed to address the distressing memories of women being abused by government employees. If I could create a solution to the systemic issues that inflicted harm to me and those I had to leave behind in the shelter … could we heal?

Do I have the qualifications to open a domestic violence shelter? I never attended college so I lack a degree—that gap in my resume is one of the main things I’m self conscious of. Growing up, I learned hospitality from my father’s small business. After high school, I went to trade school for construction. Then, while married, I started therapy. I’m no psychology major, but I’ve learned how to handle my trauma and deal with others who are still consumed by theirs.

I researched “how to open a shelter” to see if it was possible.

What kind of shelter? Domestic violence shelter.

Gender specific? Women.

Family or no children? No kids.

It could get more specific than that. During my intake, I was asked about my religion. I was scared to answer. I grew up a specific denomination of Christian. And while my personal beliefs have changed through my marriage separation, I still love my God. I didn’t want the blame for my homelessness to be put on my faith. The case worker explained there were domestic violence shelters specifically for members of certain religions. Catholicism, Judaism, Muslum. The religion I grew up in was not represented.

From this intake process to the seven months in this system, I've discovered a need.

Is it my purpose to build this safe place? That answer's not online. It's mine to find.
