A Call to Breathe Land Back to Life


by South Bronx Unite/The Land Stewards



At the edge of the Harlem River, where the air once tasted of diesel and salt, we gather. Children run through a garden that did not exist ten years ago, tracing lines in the soil where fences once stood. Music drifts from the avenue. The smell of earth mixes with exhaust and memory. This is how healing begins, not as policy, but as presence.

For generations, the South Bronx has carried the city’s hidden weight. Highways cut through our blocks; waste stations and power plants crowded our lungs. One in five of our children has asthma. Yet as one of our neighbors reminds us, nada nos han dado gratis—nothing here has ever been given for free. Every park, every garden, every mural wall has been won through collective struggle and the stubborn conviction that life itself is worth defending.

Our work moves between the practical and the poetic. We compost and we dream. We build gardens on empty lots and altars for our loved ones. We turn underpasses and abandoned waterfronts into open-air stages, and derelict buildings into anchors of neighborhood life. DJ sessions become cleanups, rituals, and rehearsals all at once. The art lives where we live. Each act opens the future a little wider. The struggle, as our elders teach, is also joy: the moment the community begins to see itself as the author of its destiny. Community here is a choreography of survival and creation.

When we speak of land, we mean more than acreage or deeds. We mean the ground that remembers who we are. It holds the footprints of those who fished at this river’s edge, of the Young Lords who reclaimed Lincoln Hospital in the name of health and dignity, of the generations who refused to disappear. Reclaiming land is an act of memory, a way of returning the body to breath.

Through our waterfront plan, we open shorelines long closed off.

Through our community land trust, we keep soil in community hands.

Through our future H.E.ARTS Center, we braid health, education, and the arts so that care circulates like water.

We invite the larger New York community to walk these reclaimed lands with us—the gardens, underpasses, and open-air spaces we continue to liberate and care for. Follow our work at @SouthBronxUnite and southbronxunite.org. We also welcome donations here, which directly benefit both the Waterfront Plan and the H.E.Arts campaign.

The South Bronx is not a wound to be healed, but a living archive of creativity and resilience. When we care for the land, art takes root. When art flourishes, community breathes. And when we breathe together, the city rediscovers its own spirit.
